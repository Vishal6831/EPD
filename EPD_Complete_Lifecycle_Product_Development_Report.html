Abstract

This report documents the end-to-end product development lifecycle of SMARTCLASS AI, an edge-computing and Internet of Things (IoT) integrated smart classroom management system engineered to eliminate manual attendance overhead, track student cognitive engagement, and maintain optimal indoor environmental and fire safety conditions in high-density educational spaces. The system addresses the fundamental limitations of conventional roll-call and cloud-tethered IoT devices, which suffer from proxy attendance, recurring subscription fees, and prohibitive privacy and bandwidth liabilities.

The engineered solution couples an ultra-low-power ESP32 microcontroller subsystem (interfacing DHT11, MQ-135, and PIR sensors with an isolated relay driver) to a local Edge AI computing node running lightweight 478-point facial landmark regression (Google MediaPipe) and 128-dimensional deep face metric learning (dlib / face_recognition). Environmental telemetry and vision analytics are consolidated seamlessly into a responsive Next.js 14 / TypeScript digital-twin dashboard over local Wi-Fi via RESTful JSON services.

Rigorous verification tests demonstrate nominal 3.30 V power rail stability ($\pm 0.3\%$ regulation error), rapid actuation latency ($42.4\text{ ms}$ vs. $100\text{ ms}$ ceiling), 99.8% packet delivery under stress, and persistent attendance registration with automated distance-to-confidence scaling ($>90\%$ confidence). The system conforms strictly to IEEE Std 29148 requirements engineering, IEC 60617 schematic standards, ISO 7200 drawing sheet metadata, and IPC-2221 Class 2 design guidelines for a 2-layer FR-4 PCB. With a total prototype Bill of Materials (BOM) cost of ₹1,848.00, SMARTCLASS AI achieves substantial commercial and economic viability for institutional deployment.

2.1 Background, Motivation, and Scope of the Product

In standard collegiate lecture halls (such as standard 57-student batch rooms at KIIT), manual roll-calling consumes up to 10 minutes per lecture hour, representing an institutional productivity loss of over 16%. Concurrently, high-density student occupancy causes rapid indoor air stagnation; $CO_2$ levels exceeding $1,000\text{ ppm}$ directly impair cognitive function and concentration, while fire/smoke hazards require instantaneous detection and automated alarm triggering.

Feedback gathered from the Phase 1 preliminary design presentation highlighted three critical engineering constraints:

  1. Edge Independence: The system must not rely on active internet connectivity or paid cloud APIs; all facial recognition and sensor processing must run across a local subnet.
  2. Attendance Integrity: The system must mark a student as present immediately upon a verified face match, persisting attendance data throughout the day without requiring students to remain frozen in front of the lens.
  3. Safety Isolation: High-voltage ventilation/fan actuation circuits must be galvanically isolated from the sensitive low-voltage 3.3 V microcontroller core.

7.1 Simulation Setup and Quantitative Results

The power regulation, anti-aliasing RC filter, and relay driver stages were simulated using LTspice XVII and Proteus 8.16. Transient analysis was executed with a $100\text{ mV}_{pp}$ $50\text{ Hz}$ ripple stimulus on the 5 V rail, evaluating LDO PSRR, inductive flyback clamping across diode `D2`, and RC filter attenuation.
